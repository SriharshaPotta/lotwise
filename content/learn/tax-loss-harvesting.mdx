Tax-loss harvesting means selling investments that are down, on purpose, so the losses cancel out gains you've taken elsewhere. Your money doesn't have to leave the market: you can buy something similar straight away. You just can't buy the same thing back within the wash-sale window.

## How it saves tax

Losses offset gains of the same kind first (short against short, long against long), then the other kind. If losses are left over after that, up to $3,000 a year comes off your ordinary income, and the rest carries forward to future years with no expiry.

## Doing it well

- Harvest losses that are worth it, not every wobble. Spreads and trading costs are real.
- Short-term losses usually go further, because they offset the gains taxed at the higher rate first.
- Keep your exposure with a replacement that's similar but not substantially identical: another company in the same sector, or a fund that tracks a different index.
- Watch every account, IRAs and automatic purchases included, for 30 days before and after each sale.

## What it isn't

Harvesting doesn't make taxes disappear. The replacement you buy starts with a lower cost basis, so more of its eventual sale will be gain. It's mostly a deferral. That's still worth having: tax paid later costs less than tax paid now, and the later gain may qualify for the long-term rate.
