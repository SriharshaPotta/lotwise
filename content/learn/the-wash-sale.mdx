Selling a stock at a loss has a silver lining: the loss can come off your taxes. The wash-sale rule is the catch. Buy the same stock back too soon and, as far as the IRS is concerned, you never really left. The loss doesn't count this year.

## The 61-day window

The window is 30 days before the sale, the day of the sale, and 30 days after it. Buy the same security anywhere inside it and the sale is a wash.

Say you sell 100 NVDA on October 15 for $12,980. You paid $14,820, so you're down $1,840. Drag the buy-back date below and watch where that loss goes.

## Where the loss goes

Outside the window, nothing surprising happens. The $1,840 lands in *Deductible this year* and lowers your tax bill.

Inside the window, the loss is disallowed. It isn't destroyed, though. It moves into the shares you bought back: their cost basis goes up by $1,840. When you eventually sell them, the higher basis means a smaller gain or a bigger loss. The deduction is postponed, not lost.

## The clock comes along too

The new shares also inherit the old ones' holding period. You'd held the first lot for 217 days, so the new lot starts life 217 days old. That matters because gains on shares held longer than a year are taxed at the lower long-term rate, and a wash sale gets you there sooner.

## What counts as buying it back

Buying the exact same shares is the obvious trigger. Buying a call option on them counts too, and so does anything the IRS would call *substantially identical*. Two index funds from different providers are a grey area people argue about.

Which account you buy in doesn't matter either. A second brokerage, your spouse's account and your IRA all count. Each broker only sees its own account, so none of them can warn you. Buying back inside an IRA is the worst case: the loss doesn't move into the new shares. It's gone for good.

## How to stay clear of it

- Wait until the 31st day after the sale to buy back. For an October 15 sale, that's November 15.
- Check every account for purchases in the 30 days *before* you sell, too.
- Pause automatic dividend reinvestment on the position you're selling.
- To stay invested in the meantime, buy something similar but not identical.

Lotwise checks the whole window, across every account you own, before you place the trade.
