Every gain has an age. Sell something you've held for a year or less and the gain is *short-term*, taxed like your paycheck. Hold it longer than a year and it turns *long-term*, taxed at a lower rate: for many people 15% instead of something like 24%.

## Where the line is

"Longer than a year" means exactly that. Buy on October 23, 2025 and the first long-term day is October 24, 2026. Selling on the anniversary itself is still short-term.

## It's a cliff, not a slope

Nothing changes gradually. Sell on October 23 and the estimate is $1,093. Sell on October 24 and it's $683. Same shares, same price, same gain: one more day of holding is worth $410.

That's why the date is worth knowing *before* you sell, especially when the gain is large and the line is close.

## When waiting isn't free

The saving assumes the price holds still, and it won't. Here, if AAPL falls more than about 5% before October 24, you'd have been better off selling now and paying the higher rate. Waiting a week for a big rate change is often reasonable. Waiting months to save a little rarely is.

## A few more details

- Losses have an age too. Short-term losses offset short-term gains first, which are the expensive kind.
- A wash sale hands the old shares' holding period to the new ones, so a new lot's clock doesn't always start at zero.
- The rates here are common ones, 24% and 15%. Yours depend on your income, but the cliff is there either way.
