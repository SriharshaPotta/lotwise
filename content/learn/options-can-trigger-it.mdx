The wash-sale rule isn't only about buying the same shares back. It covers anything *substantially identical*, and the law names one thing outright: a contract or option to acquire the stock. A call option is exactly that.

## Calls count as buying back

A call gives you the right to buy shares at a set price. Buy calls on a stock within 30 days of selling it at a loss, before or after, and the sale is a wash, as if you'd bought the shares themselves.

In the demo portfolio you bought 2 NVDA calls in Brokerage Two on October 3, at $6.40 a share with 100 shares per contract: $1,280 in all. On October 15 you're about to sell 100 NVDA in Brokerage One at a $1,840 loss. Drag the calls along the calendar.

Inside the window, the $1,840 is disallowed and added to the calls' basis, which becomes $3,120 instead of $1,280. Outside it, the loss is yours to deduct this year.

## How much of the loss?

Two contracts control 200 shares and you sold 100, so the whole loss is covered. Lotwise counts each contract as the 100 shares it controls and matches up to the number of shares you sold. The IRS hasn't spelled out every case for options, so treat partial matches as estimates.

## Where the loss goes next

The disallowed loss lives on in the calls. Sell them later and their higher basis shrinks your gain or grows your loss. Let them expire worthless and the whole basis, $1,840 included, becomes a loss at that point. Either way the deduction is delayed, not destroyed, with the exception covered in the IRA trap.

## What else can trigger it

- Calls of any expiry on the same stock, including long-dated ones.
- Selling a put that's deep in the money, which can be treated like agreeing to buy.
- The same shares, bought in any account you own.

## Staying clear

- Treat option purchases like share purchases: look 30 days either side of any loss sale.
- If you want to keep upside while the window is open, use something that isn't the same company.

Lotwise reads option trades in every account and counts them against the window before you sell.
